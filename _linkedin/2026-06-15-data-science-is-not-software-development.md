If you manage your data science teams the same way you manage your software engineering teams, you are practically guaranteeing failure.

The numbers are brutal: 80–95% of enterprise AI initiatives fail to reach production or deliver measurable ROI. That is roughly double the failure rate of traditional IT projects. And the bottleneck is not compute power, algorithmic complexity, or talent shortage.

It is systemic methodology.

Traditional SDLC is deterministic. You write code, you expect a specific output, you track progress via feature delivery. Sprint velocity measures how fast you ship.

Data Science is empirical. It is a research loop built on hypothesis testing, falsification, and data entropy. A good data scientist succeeds less than 1 in 5 times — not because they are failing, but because eliminating hypotheses IS the process working correctly.

When you force an experimental science into a rigid bi-weekly Scrum sprint, you do not get better models. You get:
- Technical debt from premature productionization of unverified hypotheses
- Burned-out teams gaming metrics to show "progress" on doomed approaches
- Millions in sunk pilot costs when the model collapses in production

The organizations that get this right — Google Brain, DeepMind, Meta FAIR — all operate on the research model internally. They publish papers about what did NOT work. They celebrate negative results. They measure progress in knowledge gained, not features shipped.

**The structural shift:** Stop treating your data scientists like a software feature factory. Start treating them like an R&D unit with a portfolio of hypotheses — knowing that most will not pay off, but the ones that do will more than compensate.
